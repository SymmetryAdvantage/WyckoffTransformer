# Does a more permissive PyXtal floor reconstruct more structures?

**No. Five factors from 1.3 down to 0.1, 750 DoF-stratified oracle genes each,
and not one arm beats the control.** The hypothesis this tested — that PyXtal's
sequential rejection sampling induces a distribution that systematically misses
the reference basin — is not supported. What the change *does* buy is cost: an
18x cheaper draw and a ~30% cheaper pre-relaxation for the same reconstruction.

Tables: [pyxtal_tolerance_sweep_tables.md](pyxtal_tolerance_sweep_tables.md),
regenerated by `scripts/run_pyxtal_tolerance_sweep.py report`.

## Why it was worth testing

`single_pyxtal` draws under `Tol_matrix(prototype="atomic", factor=1.3)`, a
floor at `0.65 * (r_a + r_b)` (the factor multiplies the covalent-radius *mean*;
see [`DEFAULT_PYXTAL_TOL_FACTOR`](../src/wyckoff_transformer/cryspr/generator.py)).
Three facts made the floor suspect and the experiment cheap:

1. **Sequential rejection sampling is a biased sampler.** PyXtal places Wyckoff
   orbits one at a time and rejects a placement against the orbits already down,
   so the *joint* distribution over free coordinates is distorted even where
   every marginal is uniform — and the DoF-reduction study checked only the
   marginals. The distortion's direction is known: it under-weights
   configurations in which all sites are simultaneously tight, which is what a
   dense real crystal is.
2. **The floor does not bound the result anyway.** 45% of the structures PyXtal
   *returns* sit below it, the worst at 0.42 in tolerance units, and only 2 of
   272 sampled violations were an atom against its own periodic image.
3. **The reason the floor existed is gone.** It was there because relaxers
   collapsed on overlapped input. NEP89's ZBL core does not: 0 failures in
   18,000 draws of the ranking-protocol cohort, 227 of them below 0.8 tolerance
   units. At factor -> 0 there are no rejections at all, so the draw *is*
   uniform, which makes this a direct test rather than a proxy.

## Design

One arm per factor in `{1.3, 1.0, 0.7, 0.4, 0.1}`, over the prepared 750-gene
cohort with an ORB-relaxed reference each, `--n-trials 3` flat so the DoF-keyed
trial schedule cannot confound the comparison, and the same three stages
throughout: `generate --pyxtal-tol-factor f` -> `prescreen` (NEP89, fixed
symmetry) -> `relax` (ORB, four stages). Scoring is `StructureMatcher` at
pymatgen's defaults against each gene's reference. Every arm sees the same genes,
so the comparison is **paired** and only the discordant pairs carry information.

## The result

| factor | contact p1 | contact median | below 0.8 | reconstructed | won / lost | McNemar *p* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.3 (control)** | 0.773 | 1.364 | 1.1% | **0.576** (432/750) | — | — |
| 1.0 | 0.670 | 1.144 | 4.2% | 0.564 (423) | 46 / 55 | 0.43 |
| 0.7 | 0.525 | 0.949 | 32.2% | 0.567 (425) | 54 / 61 | 0.58 |
| 0.4 | 0.332 | 0.836 | 47.1% | 0.545 (409) | 52 / 75 | 0.050 |
| 0.1 | **0.103** | 0.723 | **53.8%** | 0.565 (424) | 50 / 58 | 0.50 |

Flat, with a mild dip at 0.4 that points the *wrong* way and does not survive
correction for five comparisons (Bonferroni threshold 0.0125). No pattern by DoF
either: at >10 DoF the arms give 0.127 / 0.087 / 0.133 / 0.113 / 0.113, which is
noise on 150 genes.

**Two checks make the null trustworthy rather than merely quiet.**

*The manipulation worked.* Median closest contact fell 47% and the 1st
percentile 87%; at factor 0.1 more than half the draws have a contact under 0.8
tolerance units and a third under 0.5, i.e. atoms at well under a quarter of the
covalent-radius sum. That arm is a genuinely uniform, rejection-free draw.

*The experiment isolated what it claimed to.* The obvious confound was that a
permissive floor might simply let PyXtal accept *smaller cells*, in which case
the arms would differ in cell volume as well as in coordinate sampling. They do
not: the drawn per-atom volume relative to the reference is 1.154 / 1.144 /
1.143 / 1.142 / 1.139 across the five arms, flat to within 1%, with the same
p10 and p90. The factor moved coordinates and only coordinates.

So the conclusion is about the sampler, not about an artefact: **rejection-
sampling bias is not what caps reconstruction.**

## What it does buy: cost

| | factor 1.3 | factor 0.1 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| PyXtal CPU-hours | 0.256 | 0.014 | **18x cheaper** |
| PyXtal p99 per draw | **5.38 s** | 0.23 s | **23x cheaper** |
| NEP89 pre-relaxation CPU-hours | 1.262 | 0.857 | **32% cheaper** |
| ORB trials per gene | 2.08 | 2.16 | flat |
| distinct structures per gene | 2.08 | 2.16 | flat |

The draw gets cheaper because there is nothing left to reject: the 5.38 s p99 at
factor 1.3 *is* the rejection spin, and it is the heavy tail that made PyXtal
39% of the wide arm's CPU cost in the ranking protocol. The pre-relaxation gets
cheaper because a denser draw starts closer to the final density, so less
cell-contraction work remains — note this is the opposite of the within-arm
pattern, where the crowded *outliers* of a given arm are its slowest draws; the
systematic shift between arms and the outliers within one are different effects.

### One cost number in the tables is not usable

`orb_gpu_hours` and `orb_seconds_per_gene` read 1.3-2.0 for the f1.3 and f1.0
arms and 4.6-5.0 for f0.7, f0.4 and f0.1. That 3x is **measurement contention,
not a property of the arms**: other CPU work was running alongside the last
three arms, and ORB's GPU workers each need a CPU core for neighbour-list
construction, so their per-trial wall-clock inflated. The ORB *trial counts* are
sound (they are counts, not timings) and show the arms doing equal expensive
work, which is what the design intends. Do not quote the ORB timings.

## Recommendation

**Leave the default at 1.3.** Nothing in the reconstruction data justifies
changing it, and the only nominally significant cell is a degradation.

**Consider lowering it for the wide-then-narrow arm specifically**, where the
draw is multiplied tenfold and PyXtal's rejection spin therefore dominates: 0.7
gives an 12x cheaper draw and a 37% cheaper pre-relaxation at a reconstruction
rate statistically indistinguishable from the control (0.567 vs 0.576, *p* =
0.58). That is a cost argument, not a quality one, and it should be made as one.

## Limitations

- **One potential, one matcher.** Reconstruction is judged by ORB plus
  `StructureMatcher` at pymatgen's defaults; a looser tolerance would find more
  matches in every arm and could in principle reorder them.
- **A flat 3-trial budget.** Chosen to remove the schedule as a confound, so
  these rates are not comparable to the ranking protocol's DoF-keyed ones.
- **The null is about the *floor*, not about PyXtal's sampler in general.**
  Removing rejections removes one specific bias. The placement order, the volume
  estimator and the cell-shape draw are untouched, and the oracle study says the
  cell is worth as much as the coordinates — see
  [the search ideas](de_novo_search_ideas.md).
- **Crowding beyond a point plausibly hurts.** The f0.4 dip is not significant,
  but it is in the direction one would expect if over-crowded starts relax into
  denser wrong structures rather than into the reference.
